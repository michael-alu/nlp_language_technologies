## Naive Bayes experiments

Repeated stratified 5-fold CV with three repeats selected ComplementNB with TF-IDF unigrams + bigrams and alpha=0.01. Its development macro-F1 was **0.5325 ± 0.0217**, versus **0.5169 ± 0.0070** for MNB at identical features and alpha. This is a descriptive improvement; overlapping CV training sets mean these SDs do not establish statistical significance.

At alpha=1, MNB scored **0.5111 ± 0.0078** on word counts, **0.3970 ± 0.0086** on TF-IDF unigrams and **0.3798 ± 0.0071** after adding bigrams. Thus TF-IDF and phrases did not improve this baseline under the default smoothing. Lowering alpha to 0.01 substantially improved TF-IDF bigram MNB. A plausible explanation is that L2-normalized TF-IDF and the expanded bigram vocabulary change the scale of feature evidence relative to additive smoothing. This is an interpretation, not a separate causal test. EDA §6’s sparse, long-tailed vocabulary supports examining this sensitivity; EDA §7’s strong topic words explains why simple counts remain competitive.

ComplementNB at alpha=0.01 achieved CV international F1 **0.0756 ± 0.0963**; MNB at the same alpha had zero international F1. All ten candidates had zero entertainment F1 across CV. The complement formulation therefore showed only limited rare-class gains in development data, consistent with testing rather than assuming the imbalance motivation of [Rennie et al. (2003)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jrennie/papers/icml03-nb.pdf).

The deterministic baselines each have one fixed-test result; their variability is reported through development CV, not fabricated seed repeats. Logistic regression's selected configuration has CV macro-F1 **0.5627 ± 0.0134**. Neural seed variation and CV fold variation measure different things and should not be pooled.

Logistic regression and Naive Bayes have similar weighted F1, yet their macro-F1 differs substantially. The per-class table explains much of this gap: international F1 is **0.4615** for logistic regression and **0.0000** for Naive Bayes, while both have entertainment F1 **0.5000**. Naive Bayes is slightly stronger on national and sports F1, so accuracy alone obscures its rare-class weakness. EDA §3’s class imbalance and §7’s overlapping national/international topics make this distinction particularly relevant.

Entertainment test F1=0.5 should not be interpreted as reliable recovery: the test set contains only three entertainment articles, versus eight international articles. The zero entertainment CV mean alongside this nonzero test score illustrates sensitivity to which rare examples are held out. The fixed-test result and development CV estimate also use different holdout compositions and training sizes, so their difference is not evidence of a general improvement.

The linear baseline exceeds Naive Bayes on macro-F1 in both development CV and the fixed test. EDA §4 describes long articles (median around 270 words). This is compatible with [Wang and Manning (2012)](https://aclanthology.org/P12-2018/), who found SVMs stronger than NB on longer reviews and NB stronger on short sentiment snippets. Our classifier, language and topic task differ; the comparison does not prove document length caused the gap. Their task-dependent bigram findings also caution against assuming phrase features help every news dataset.

The transformer has stronger mean weighted F1 and lower log loss, but macro-F1 **0.7043 ± 0.1043** spans substantial seed variation. Its mean is close to the logistic regression fixed-test score; these results do not justify a confident macro-F1 ranking. Its international F1 is **0.4794 ± 0.2466**, and entertainment F1 **0.3333 ± 0.2887**. ## MasakhaNEWS context

[MasakhaNEWS Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.09972) reports count-based Naive Bayes Swahili **76.6 weighted-F1 points**, averaged over five runs. Our selected Naive Bayes weighted F1 is **85.55 points**; our macro-F1 is **61.82 points**. Only the weighted metric is aligned for contextual comparison. Different datasets, class distributions, labels, splits, article conventions, preprocessing and selected NB configurations prevent treating the numerical gap as an improvement over that benchmark.
