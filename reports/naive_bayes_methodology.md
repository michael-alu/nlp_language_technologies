# Methodology: Model 2 — Naive Bayes

We evaluate Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB) as a classical Swahili news baseline. All runs use the shared cleaned, stratified data splits and five label IDs in `config.LABELS` order. We wrap word vectorization and classification in a scikit-learn Pipeline returned by `build_model(settings)`, so the vocabulary and inverse document frequencies are fitted only on each training partition.

We first compare unigram CountVectorizer + MNB against unigram TfidfVectorizer + MNB at alpha=1. We then add word bigrams to TF-IDF at the same alpha. Finally, we compare MNB and ComplementNB on identical unigram-plus-bigram TF-IDF features at alpha ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}. The alpha=1 MNB bigram candidate is reused rather than logged twice. These ten configurations isolate weighting, phrase features, smoothing, and the complement formulation through matched comparisons.

Vectorizers lowercase text and retain the default word token pattern (tokens of at least two characters), min_df=1 and max_df=1.0, with no stopword list or vocabulary cap. TF-IDF uses raw term frequencies, smoothed IDF and L2 normalization. MNB learns empirical class priors; ComplementNB uses norm=False. Alpha supplies additive smoothing to token estimates. TF-IDF is nonnegative and compatible with these estimators, but its fractional weights are an empirical alternative to literal multinomial counts.

Every configuration is fitted on train and scored on validation, and independently evaluated using the template’s repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation with three repeats and seed 42 on train + validation. A fresh Pipeline is fitted within each of the 15 folds. We log accuracy, log loss, macro-F1, weighted F1 and per-class F1; CV summaries include their means and sample standard deviations. Selection uses highest CV mean macro-F1, with stable experiment order resolving exact ties. These are development estimates used for tuning, not unbiased final test estimates. Fold SD describes variability across dependent resamples, not a confidence interval.

The selected configuration is refitted on train only, consistent with the existing logistic regression final protocol, and evaluated once on the untouched fixed test split. The selected settings and their CV provenance are logged with the final test metrics. We save confusion/ROC plots, misclassified articles and Zindi probability submissions. Naive Bayes is deterministic for fixed data and settings, so repeated seed runs would not supply independent evidence; neural model final seed means and SDs are reported separately. Only 3 entertainment and 8 international articles occur in the test set, limiting precision of rare-class comparisons.

The EDA’s frequent function words and distinctive topic words motivate TF-IDF and a bag-of-words baseline (§7); its long-tailed Swahili vocabulary motivates smoothing (§6); its imbalance motivates ComplementNB (§3). Rennie et al. (2003) propose complement estimates to mitigate skewed training data. Wang and Manning (2012) report task-dependent bigram effects and stronger SVM performance on long reviews than NB; this motivates a comparison with our linear baseline without assuming that their result must transfer to Swahili news.

Cross-model reporting is implemented in `notebooks/07_results_comparison.ipynb` through `experiment_log.load_all_experiments()`. It exports a five-row final test table, macro-F1 bar chart, per-class table/heatmap and full CV table. Pending models are explicitly marked. Final Results and Discussion must be completed after all five final records are available. MasakhaNEWS uses weighted F1 on a different Swahili dataset; its Table 3 count-based NB result is 76.6 weighted-F1 points, averaged across five runs. This provides context rather than a directly comparable benchmark for our macro-F1.
